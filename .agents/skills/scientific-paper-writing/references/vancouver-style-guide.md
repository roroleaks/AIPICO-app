# Vancouver Style (ICMJE / NLM) Reference Guide

This reference guide provides formatting standards and examples according to the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) and the National Library of Medicine (NLM).

---

## 1. General Principles

1. **Sequential Numbering:** Citations are numbered consecutively in the order in which they are first mentioned in the text.
2. **In-Text Marker:** Arabic numerals in square brackets, e.g., `[1]`, `[1, 2]`, or `[1-4]`.
3. **Reference List Order:** The reference list at the end of the paper is arranged in numerical order corresponding to the in-text numbers.
4. **Author Names:**
   - List the author's surname followed by initials without periods between initials (e.g., `Owen DD`, `Smith AB`).
   - If there are 1 to 6 authors, list all authors separated by commas.
   - If there are more than 6 authors, list the first 6 authors followed by `, et al.`
5. **Article Title:** Sentence case (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Do NOT use quotation marks. End with a period.
6. **Journal Abbreviation:** Use the standard NLM abbreviation (Index Medicus), followed by a period (e.g., `Am J Obstet Gynecol.`, `BJOG.`, `Obstet Gynecol.`, `Ultrasound Obstet Gynecol.`).
7. **Year and Publication Details:** `Year;Volume(Issue):Pages.` or `Year.`
8. **Digital Identifiers:** Include the DOI (`doi:10.xxxx/...`) and PubMed URL (`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/.../`).

## 2. Standard Reference Examples

### A. Journal Article (1 to 6 Authors)
```text
1. Berghella V, Paladino I, Gulersen M, Roman A. Preoperative considerations and outcomes for cervical cerclage in high-risk pregnancies. AJOG MFM. 2026;8(2):102054. doi:10.1016/j.ajogmf.2026.102054 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42425371/
```

### B. Journal Article (> 6 Authors)
```text
2. Owen J, Hankins G, Iams JD, Berghella V, Sheffield JS, Perez-Delboy A, et al. Multicenter randomized controlled trial of cerclage for preterm birth prevention in high-risk women with shortened midtrimester cervical length. Am J Obstet Gynecol. 2009;201(4):375.e1-375.e8. doi:10.1016/j.ajog.2009.08.015 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19800317/
```

### C. Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
```text
3. Conde-Agudelo A, Romero R. Vaginal progesterone for preventing preterm birth and adverse perinatal outcomes in singleton gestations with a short cervix: a meta-analysis of individual patient data. Am J Obstet Gynecol. 2018;218(2):161-180. doi:10.1016/j.ajog.2017.11.576 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29155152/
```

### D. Clinical Practice Guideline
```text
4. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. Prediction and prevention of spontaneous preterm birth: ACOG Practice Bulletin, Number 234. Obstet Gynecol. 2021;138(2):e65-e90. doi:10.1097/AOG.0000000000004479 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34293771/
```

## 3. In-Text Citation Patterns

- **Single source assertion:**
  `Transvaginal ultrasound measurement of cervical length is the most predictive screening modality for preterm birth [1].`
- **Multiple source corroboration:**
  `Randomized trials have demonstrated a significant reduction in preterm birth before 34 weeks [1, 3].`
- **Range of consecutive sources:**
  `Several international trials corroborate these findings [1-4].`
- **Narrative attribution:**
  `Conde-Agudelo and Romero [3] confirmed that vaginal progesterone reduced composite neonatal morbidity by 35%.`
